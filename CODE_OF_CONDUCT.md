# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want Vishwakarma to be a project where people do their best work. That requires an
environment where contributors are treated as colleagues regardless of experience level,
background, identity, employer, or how loudly they can argue.

We pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for
everyone.

## What good participation looks like

Assume the person you are replying to is competent and acting in good faith. Most
disagreements in software are disagreements about context, not about intelligence.

Critique the work, not the person. "This re-renders on every keystroke" is useful.
"This is amateur code" is not, and it also says less.

Be specific. A review comment that identifies the exact line and the exact failure mode
saves everyone a round trip. A vague expression of dissatisfaction costs one.

Accept that maintainers will sometimes say no. A rejected contribution is not a
rejection of the contributor, and a project that accepts everything becomes a project
worth using for nothing.

Give credit. If someone's idea shaped your patch, say so in the pull request.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome personal attention.

Personal insults, and derogatory comments about someone's identity, nationality,
religion, gender, sexuality, disability, or appearance.

Sustained disruption of discussions, deliberate intimidation, and following someone
across threads to continue an argument they have disengaged from.

Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.

Dismissing a contributor on the basis of their tooling, their English fluency, their
seniority, or their use of AI assistance. This project is *about* AI-assisted
engineering; gatekeeping on that basis is incoherent as well as unkind.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues and pull requests, in any project
communication channel, and when representing the project in public spaces.

## Enforcement

Report conduct issues by opening a confidential report to the maintainers, or by
emailing the address listed in the repository's SECURITY.md. Reports are handled
privately. You will get an acknowledgement, and you will be told the outcome.

Maintainers will respond proportionately. The available responses, roughly in order,
are: a private clarification of expectations, a public correction, a temporary
suspension from project spaces, and a permanent ban. Serious violations may skip
straight to the end of that list.

Maintainers who violate this code are subject to it in the same way, with the difference
that a maintainer's conduct problem is more damaging and will generally be treated more
seriously, not less.

## Attribution
